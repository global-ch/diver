# Frequently Asked Questions

## General Questions

**Q: What age do I need to be to learn scuba diving?**
A: Most organizations allow certification starting at age 10 for younger divers with limited depths, and age 15+ for full certification. Adults can start at any age if physically fit.

**Q: How long does Open Water certification take?**
A: Typically 3-4 days. Some agencies offer accelerated programs (2-3 days) or extended learning options.

**Q: Can I get certified in a pool before diving in the ocean?**
A: Yes! Many courses include confined water training in pools before open water dives. Some agencies offer "Pool to Sea" certifications.

**Q: Do I need to be a good swimmer to scuba dive?**
A: You should be comfortable in water, but you don't need to be a competitive swimmer. Fins make propulsion much easier than swimming.

**Q: How long is my certification valid?**
A: Certifications don't expire, but it's recommended to dive regularly and take refresher courses if you haven't dived in 6+ months.

## Physical & Health Questions

**Q: Can I dive if I have asthma?**
A: This requires medical clearance from a diving medicine physician. Each case is individual.

**Q: Can I dive while pregnant?**
A: Diving is not recommended during pregnancy due to risks of decompression sickness to the fetus. Most agencies advise against it.

**Q: Can I dive with diabetes?**
A: Yes, with proper management and medical clearance. Work with your doctor and instructor to create a safe diving plan.

**Q: What if I have claustrophobia?**
A: Scuba diving is open water with good visibility in most cases. However, cave or wreck diving may not be suitable. Start with recreational diving and see how you feel.

**Q: Can I dive if I'm overweight?**
A: Yes, your fitness level is more important than weight. Work with a certified instructor who can help with equipment sizing and buoyancy control.

**Q: How soon after flying can I dive?**
A: Wait at least 12 hours after flying before diving. Diving and flying too close together increases decompression sickness risk.

## Equipment Questions

**Q: Should I buy my own equipment or rent?**
A: Beginners should rent until confident they'll continue diving. Buy personal items (mask, fins, regulator) for hygiene; other items can be rented.

**Q: What's the difference between a wetsuit and a drysuit?**
A: Wetsuits allow water in and rely on a thin layer to insulate. Drysuits keep you completely dry with additional insulation layers.

**Q: How much do I need to spend on equipment?**
A: Basic personal equipment (mask, fins, snorkel): $100-300. Full equipment setup: $1,000-3,000+. Rentals: $15-50 per item per dive.

**Q: How do I know what size wetsuit to get?**
A: Follow manufacturer sizing charts based on height and weight. Try on before buying when possible.

**Q: Can I use my own regulator rented equipment?**
A: Yes, you can mix and match. Many divers own personal regulators for hygiene and comfort.

## Diving Safety Questions

**Q: What should I do if I run low on air?**
A: Perform an immediate ascent with your buddy. This is why air monitoring is crucial. Plan dives conservatively.

**Q: What is nitrogen narcosis?**
A: A condition where nitrogen becomes intoxicating at depths, similar to alcohol intoxication. Happens around 30+ meters for most people.

**Q: What is decompression sickness (the bends)?**
A: Nitrogen bubbles form in tissues when ascending too quickly. Prevent by ascending slowly and making safety stops.

**Q: Should I dive after consuming alcohol?**
A: No. Alcohol affects judgment, dehydrates you, and increases decompression sickness risk. Avoid for 12+ hours before diving.

**Q: What do I do if I panic underwater?**
A: Stop, take deep breaths, and remember you have air in your tank. Signal your buddy for help. Training prepares you for this.

**Q: Is it safe to dive alone?**
A: No. Always use the buddy system. Diving with a partner provides safety and emergency support.

## Location & Planning Questions

**Q: What's the best place to learn diving?**
A: Warm, calm waters with good visibility. Popular beginner destinations: Caribbean, Hawaii, Mexico, Southeast Asia.

**Q: When is the best time to dive?**
A: This depends on location. Generally, shoulder seasons (spring/fall) offer good conditions and fewer crowds.

**Q: How much does a dive trip cost?**
A: Budget $100-300 per dive including boat/guide fees. Certification courses: $300-500. Accommodations and travel vary greatly.

**Q: Can I dive if I wear glasses?**
A: Yes, get a prescription mask with corrective lenses. Your instructor can help you arrange this.

**Q: What if I get seasick on the boat?**
A: Sea bands (acupressure bands) or medication like dramamine can help. Eat light meals and focus on the horizon.

## Species & Environmental Questions

**Q: Are sharks dangerous to divers?**
A: Shark attacks on divers are extremely rare. Most sharks avoid humans. Follow guides' instructions and don't touch.

**Q: What should I do if I encounter a sea creature?**
A: Observe from a distance and don't touch. Most marine life avoids humans. Photographers should use telephoto lenses.

**Q: How can I help protect coral reefs?**
A: Avoid touching coral, use reef-safe sunscreen, don't leave trash, and don't collect shells or marine life.

**Q: Is underwater photography allowed?**
A: Yes, but check local regulations. Minimize impact on marine life; never chase or disturb animals for photos.

## Advanced Questions

**Q: What's the difference between recreational and technical diving?**
A: Recreational diving follows safe limits with no decompression obligations. Technical diving goes deeper/longer and requires decompression stops.

**Q: What is cave diving?**
A: Diving in underwater caves with overhead environments. Requires specialized training and is not for beginners.

**Q: Can I dive from a beach or do I need a boat?**
A: Both! Shore diving is often easier to arrange and less expensive. Boat diving reaches different sites and depths.

**Q: What certification do I need for deep diving?**
A: Advanced Open Water and Deep Diving specialty certifications are recommended for dives beyond 40 meters (130 feet).
