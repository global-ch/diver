# Diving Glossary

A comprehensive alphabetical list of scuba diving terms and definitions.

## A

**Air Integration** - Technology that wirelessly monitors tank pressure on your dive computer.

**Alternate Air Source** - A second regulator or octopus for emergency air sharing with a buddy.

**Ambient Pressure** - The total pressure at a specific depth, including surface atmospheric pressure.

**Ascent** - Swimming upward toward the surface after diving.

**Atmospheric Pressure** - The pressure exerted by the earth's atmosphere at sea level.

## B

**Barotrauma** - Physical damage to body tissues caused by pressure changes (most common: ear barotrauma).

**Buddy System** - The practice of diving with a partner who stays close for safety and emergency assistance.

**BCD (Buoyancy Control Device)** - An inflatable vest that allows you to add or remove air to control buoyancy underwater.

**Bends (Decompression Sickness/DCS)** - A serious condition caused by nitrogen bubbles forming in the body due to rapid pressure reduction.

**Benthic Zone** - The area at the bottom of a body of water.

**Bleed Off** - To slowly release air from a tank or BCD.

## C

**Certification** - Official documentation that you've completed diving training and can dive independently.

**Cold Water Diving** - Diving in water below 15°C (59°F), typically requiring special training and equipment.

**Confined Water** - A controlled environment like a pool used for initial diving training.

**Current** - Horizontal movement of water that can carry divers if not properly managed.

**Cylinder** - The metal tank that holds compressed air (also called a tank).

## D

**Decompression** - The gradual reduction of pressure on the body during ascent.

**Decompression Sickness (DCS)** - See "Bends."

**Decompression Stop (Safety Stop)** - A mandatory pause during ascent to allow nitrogen to be eliminated from the body.

**Depth Gauge** - An instrument that measures depth underwater.

**Descent** - Swimming downward into deeper water.

**Divemaster** - A professional-level diver who assists instructors and leads dives.

**Drysuit** - A waterproof suit that keeps divers completely dry by preventing water from entering.

## E

**Equalization** - The process of balancing pressure in your ears, sinuses, and other air spaces during descent.

**Eustachian Tube** - A tube connecting the middle ear to the throat, used during equalization.

**Exposure Suit** - A suit worn to protect against temperature loss and abrasion (includes wetsuits and drysuits).

## F

**Fins** - Footgear worn to improve swimming efficiency and mobility underwater.

**First Stage** - The part of the regulator that attaches to the tank and reduces pressure.

**Freediving** - Diving on a single breath without scuba equipment.

## G

**Gauge** - An instrument measuring pressure, depth, or other diving parameters.

**Gradient Factor** - A variable in dive computer algorithms related to allowable nitrogen levels.

**Grotto** - A large underwater cave formation (often a tourism dive site).

## H

**Hazmat** - Hazardous materials; avoid diving where hazmat may be present.

**Head-First Entry** - A diving entry technique performed by entering headfirst into the water.

**Hydrostatic Pressure** - Pressure exerted by water due to its weight and depth.

## I

**Indicator** - A gauge showing tank pressure, battery level, or other important information.

**Inert Gas Narcosis** - See "Nitrogen Narcosis."

**Inflator/Deflator** - The mechanism on a BCD for adding or removing air.

**Instructor** - A certified professional who trains new divers.

## J

**Jack Knife Entry** - A diving entry technique performed by bending at the waist.

## K

**Kelp** - Seaweed found in cold and temperate ocean environments.

## L

**Laser** - Light amplification by stimulated emission of radiation; used in some dive lights.

**Lateral Line** - A sensory system in fish that detects movement and pressure changes.

**LDSD** - Low Dilution Scuba Diving.

## M

**Mask** - The device worn over the eyes to provide clear vision underwater.

**Mouthpiece** - The part of the regulator that goes in your mouth.

**Maximum Operating Depth (MOD)** - The deepest depth to which a specific gas mixture can safely be used.

**Nitrogen Narcosis** - A condition similar to intoxication caused by nitrogen at depth; also called "rapture of the deep."

## N

**Neutral Buoyancy** - The state where you neither sink nor float; perfect buoyancy control.

**NITROX** - A gas mixture with higher oxygen and lower nitrogen than regular air.

**Nitrogen** - A gas that makes up about 78% of air and becomes narcotic at depth.

**Normal Operating Depth** - The typical recommended depth for comfortable, safe diving.

## O

**Octopus (Alternate Air Source)** - A second regulator available for buddy air sharing.

**Open Water Diver** - Entry-level scuba certification allowing independent diving to 18 meters.

**Oxygen** - The gas that provides breathable air; makes up about 21% of air.

**Oxygen Toxicity** - A dangerous condition caused by excessive oxygen exposure at depth.

## P

**Partial Pressure** - The pressure contributed by a specific gas in a gas mixture.

**Padi** - Professional Association of Diving Instructors (major certification organization).

**Pelagic Zone** - The open water column of an ocean or lake.

**Pounds Per Square Inch (PSI)** - A unit of pressure measurement.

**Primary Regulator** - Your main regulator delivering breathing gas from the tank.

## Q

**Quarry** - A human-made diving location formed by excavation.

## R

**Rec Limit** - The recreational diving depth limit (usually 40 meters).

**Regulator** - The device that reduces tank pressure and delivers breathable air.

**Relief Valve** - A valve that allows air to escape if pressure becomes excessive.

**Repetitive Dive** - A dive performed after a previous dive with minimal surface interval.

**Reverse Block** - Sinus or middle ear barotrauma during ascent due to inability to equalize.

## S

**Safety Stop** - See "Decompression Stop."

**Saturation Diving** - Extended diving where divers remain at depth in special habitats.

**Second Stage** - The part of the regulator that delivers air directly to the diver.

**Semi-Closed Circuit Rebreather (SCR)** - A rebreather system that recycles some breathing gases.

**Shallow Water Diving** - Recreational diving in water 5-12 meters deep.

**Snorkel** - A tube allowing breathing while at the surface with your face in the water.

**Squeeze** - Barotrauma caused by pressure on enclosed air spaces.

**Staging** - A dive technique where divers stop at specific depths.

**Streamlined** - Configuration of diving equipment for minimal drag and effort.

## T

**Tank** - See "Cylinder."

**Technical Diving** - Advanced diving using specialized equipment, gases, and procedures.

**Thermocline** - A layer of water where temperature changes rapidly with depth.

**Trimmed Out** - Proper body position and weight distribution for efficient underwater movement.

## U

**Ultraviolet (UV)** - Radiation beyond visible light; affects underwater colors with depth.

**Undercurrent** - A water current that pulls divers toward the bottom.

**Unidirectional** - One-way flow; used in valve descriptions.

## V

**Valve** - A device controlling air flow in regulators or tanks.

**Venturi Effect** - A vacuum effect that enhances breathing ease on regulators at depth.

**Visual Diving** - Diving using natural light sources during daytime hours.

## W

**Warm Water Diving** - Diving in tropical or subtropical waters above 25°C (77°F).

**Water Temperature** - A critical factor determining exposure protection needed.

**Weight Belt** - A belt with removable weights for achieving proper buoyancy.

**Wetsuit** - A suit with water circulation allowing a thin water layer to insulate the diver.

**Whiteout** - Condition caused by suspended particles reducing visibility.

**Working Pressure** - The safe operating pressure range for diving equipment.

## X

**Xenon** - A rare inert gas used in some high-tech diving applications.

## Y

**Y-Valve** - A manifold connecting two tanks to a single regulator.

## Z

**Zodiac** - A rigid-hull inflatable boat commonly used to transport divers.

**Zeolite** - A desiccant used in some diving equipment to remove moisture.

## Common Abbreviations

- **ATA** - Atmospheres Absolute
- **BAR** - Unit of pressure (1 BAR ≈ 14.5 PSI)
- **BCD** - Buoyancy Control Device
- **DCS** - Decompression Sickness
- **FFEF** - Fitness For Duty
- **IDD** - Infection Disease Diving
- **MOD** - Maximum Operating Depth
- **NITROX** - Nitrogen-Oxygen blend
- **OW** - Open Water
- **PFD** - Personal Flotation Device
- **PSI** - Pounds Per Square Inch
- **RDF** - Rebreather Decompression
- **SAC** - Surface Air Consumption
- **SSI** - Scuba Schools International
- **TDI** - Technical Diving International
- **UHMS** - Undersea Hyperbaric Medical Society
- **USD** - Unified Standard Diving
- **UTC** - Underwater Technical Certification
